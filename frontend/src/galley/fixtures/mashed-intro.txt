Heavy machinery forms the operational backbone of mechanized mining because it supports large-scale excavation, drilling, crushing, loading, and transportation required to sustain mineral production. Excavators, drilling rigs, conveyers, loaders, haul trucks, and other mobile and stationary equipment operate in close proximity to workers within dynamic and often constrained mining environments[1], [2]. Despite continuing advances in automation and autonomous technologies, many mining operations still rely on conventional human-operated machinery, particularly where financial, technological, and infrastructural conditions constrain the adoption of automated systems [3], [4]. This continued reliance on heavy machinery creates persistent human-machine interaction (HMI) challenges, as the high power, large size, mobility, and limited visibility of mining equipment can increase the consequences of operational errors [5]. Thus, improving the safety of interactions between workers and heavy machinery is essential for reducing machinery-related accidents as mining operations gradually advance toward increased automation [3]. The use of heavy machinery exposes workers to various hazards, including equipment failures, collisions, struck-by incidents, reversing and maneuvering, blind zones, and risks associated with dumping, loading, and haulage activities [6], [7]. The occurrences and severity of these hazards are influenced by the interaction of human, machinery, environmental, organizational, and operational conditions. [8], [9] . Similarly, equipment conditions, maintenance, visibility, and warning systems influence safety of workers when interacting with machinery. Dust, noise, lighting, road and ground conditions, mine layout, and transport patterns may further influence visibility, communication, and equipment maneuverability [10]. Organizational practices, coordination, supervision, and exposure to hazardous machinery also contribute to machinery-related safety outcomes[11]. Thus, machinery-related safety management requires a comprehensive approach that considers the combined conditions under which workers interact with machinery.  Despite these challenges, heavy machinery is essential for mechanized mining for high-volume material handling, reducing workers’ physical burden, and improving operational productivity and efficiency [12]. The continued use of machinery highlights the need to strengthen safety in human-machine interaction, particularly in environments where transition towards automation is gradual[5]. This requires a systematic identification of related hazards, effective operator training, regular equipment inspection and maintenance, adequate visibility and warning systems, and suitable organizational, environmental, and risk-control measures[13], [14]. Addressing these factors in an integrated manner can improve safety while keeping the operational advantages of mechanization during the gradual transitions towards automation. However, their combined influence on HMI safety and effect on safety outcomes have received limited attention, highlighting the need for a more integrated framework for managing machinery-related safety [3]. Accordingly, this study develops an HMI safety risk management framework to investigate the factors influencing HMI safety performance and their relationship with safety outcomes in mining operations. PLS-SEM is employed to empirically assess the relationships among the identified factors, while Bayesian Network analysis is used to represent the probabilistic dependencies and evaluate variations in safety states under different conditions [15], [16], [17]. By integrating these complementary approaches, the study provides empirical evidence of relationships affecting HMI safety and a probabilistic basis for assessing their implications for safety outcomes. The proposed framework offers a systematic approach for identifying critical safety influencing conditions, evaluating their integrated effects on human-machine interactions, and supporting risk-informed safety management and intervention planning in mechanized mining operations.
